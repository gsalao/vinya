# Looking after the Vinya website

This is for whoever runs the studio. It assumes nothing technical.

You can change every word and every picture on the site yourself. Nothing you can
type here will break it.

## Getting in

Go to **vinyayoga.com/admin** and sign in.

If you forget your password, whoever set this up can reset it for you.

## How a change reaches the site

You edit something and press **Save**. The change is kept straight away, and the
site starts updating itself within a few seconds. About a minute later it is
live.

The coloured strip at the top tells you where it has got to:

| It says | It means |
| --- | --- |
| Saved. Starting to publish… | Your change is safe and on its way |
| Updating your site — about a minute… | It is rebuilding |
| **Your changes are live** | Done. There is a link to go and look |
| Anything in red | Something needs your attention — read on |

You can close the laptop after saving. It carries on without you.

## If something cannot be published

The site does not change. It carries on showing the last version that worked,
and the strip tells you which row to fix and what to type instead. For example:

> events, row 2: month reads "2026-09-05" but must read like "September 2026".

Fix that box, save again, and it goes out. **You cannot break the site by typing
the wrong thing** — the worst that happens is your change does not appear until
you correct it.

## Where things are

The menu across the top matches the pages of your website.

| Page | What you will find |
| --- | --- |
| **Home** | The big headline, the pictures, the quotes, the partner logos |
| **Classes** | Your classes, the timetable, prices, offerings, the FAQ |
| **Teachers** | Teacher profiles and photos |
| **Events** | Upcoming gatherings, and past ones |
| **About** | The story, the founder, the studios you teach at |
| **Settings** | Who gets booking emails, who can sign in, your password |

Anything that appears in two places is edited once, and says so — *"also shown on
the Home page"*.

## Adding and removing

Most sections have **Add another** at the bottom and **Remove** on each row. The
small arrows on the left move a row up or down; that order is the order visitors
see.

The site rearranges itself to suit. Add a fourth partner logo and the row starts
scrolling gently instead of wrapping. Add a second teacher and arrows appear so
visitors can flip between you. Take one away and the rest close the gap.

## Pictures

Press **Choose a photo** and pick one — straight from your phone is fine, it gets
made smaller automatically before it uploads.

Then **drag the picture** in the little preview to say what should stay in view.
This matters because the same photo is a tall shape on one page and a wide band
on another, so something always gets cut off. Dragging decides what. Arrow keys
work too if that is easier.

**Remove** takes a picture off the site and deletes it properly, so it stops
using up space.

## Things you cannot change, and why

**Prices show their payment link greyed out.** You can change what a pass is
called, what it costs, and its note — but not where the money goes. A QR code
cannot be checked by eye, so if that link could be edited here, anyone who got
into this screen could quietly send payments somewhere else.

**The "next gathering" on the home page** is simply your first upcoming event.
Change the event and the home page follows. It used to be typed separately and
the two drifted apart, so now it cannot.

**Adding a new photo file for a partner logo** works, but the logo files
themselves are best given to your developer if you want a new one prepared
properly.

## Settings

**Who receives booking requests** — add or change the addresses. When you change
this, whoever was receiving them before gets an email saying so. That is
deliberate: it means nobody can quietly redirect your enquiries.

**Who can sign in** — add someone by giving their email and a starting password,
and confirming your own password. Tell them their password yourself and ask them
to change it. Everyone has the same access. You can remove people, including
yourself — that signs you out. The last remaining account cannot be removed, or
nobody could ever get back in.

**Your password** — yours only. Nobody can change anyone else's.

## When to call your developer

Almost never, ideally. Worth calling about:

- The strip says a technical problem stopped the site updating
- Nothing has happened five minutes after saving
- The site is down, slow, or looks wrong in a way you did not cause
- You want a new page, a new kind of section, or a design change

Not worth calling about: anything you can type. That is the whole point.
